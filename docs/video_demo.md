# CareMatch recording guide

Allow about 90 seconds for the core story. This is a suggested cut, independent of the contest’s video limit.

| Time | On screen | Suggested narration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–10 s | Open **Find care**. Choose **Dialysis** and enter **Delhi**. | “A health worker needs to choose which branch to call. A nearby listing can mention dialysis without giving enough evidence about the service.” |
| 10–30 s | Search. In **Best evidence**, open the comparison and point to one exact facility quote. Then choose **Nearest**. | “Dashmesh comes first on evidence at 9.2 kilometres. Moolchand is 7.6 kilometres from the Delhi search centre and has Moderate evidence. That saves 1.6 kilometres on this map. A city is an approximate starting point, so the worker can enter a PIN or coordinates.” |
| 30–42 s | Choose **Compare listed prices**. Point to the explanation of the fallback. | “Here the records give us too few comparable patient prices. We keep the nearby options and say what the worker needs to confirm by phone.” |
| 42–52 s | Search **Burn care** in **Port Blair**. Show the empty result and the separately labelled distant option. | “For this island search, no facility meets our evidence rule within 300 kilometres. Sending someone to an unsupported listing would waste the very time this tool is meant to save.” |
| 52–78 s | Open **Verification map**, leave the budget at **3**, then tick **The first branch answered no**. | “For a coordinator, the decision is which branches to verify first. Accounting for overlap, three positive calls would add 365 unique PIN areas to documented 50-kilometre coverage. Picking the three largest individual gains adds 152. If the first branch says no, the remaining two calls could still add 258.” |
| 78–90 s | Finish on the recalculated plan. | “The same records support two decisions: where a health worker should call today, and where a planner can make the next referral more reliable. Both decisions can be traced back to a facility record.” |

## Before recording

- Open the app and run one search for each service you will show. The first search for a service prepares its evidence
  and takes two to three seconds; later searches are immediate.
- Scroll with the pointer over the result list. Over the map, the wheel zooms the map.
- The verification map redraws 14,008 points: wait about five seconds after each change before speaking over it.
- After the first search the page header steps aside, so the result heading and the first card are on the first screen
  at 1440 × 900.
- A misspelled place is safe: “Banglore” offers “Bengaluru, Karnataka” as a button.

For Q&A, use **How we decided**, the service verification lead model, and the travel-assumption page. A PIN count measures geographic reach from PIN centres. It does not count patients, road-time savings or newly constructed services. The held-out lead model predicts documentary evidence and is distinct from the coverage optimization.

The offline comparison demo uses saved outputs from the 38 evaluation searches. The single-file notebook reproduces the calculations from the supplied tables.
